Closed vineyards record how persistent homology changes along a periodic family of filtrations. Their topology can be surprisingly complicated, with recent work showing that vines may exhibit nontrivial monodromy even in very simple settings, and more generally that every link can occur in a closed vineyard. In this paper, we study how the global topology and structure of a vineyard change when the periodic family of functions changes. We study radial persistence for a generic planar curve $\M$, where an observation point $x\in\mathbb{R}^2$ defines the squared-distance function $d_\mathbb{E}^2 (\cdot ,x)|_\M$. Moving the point $x$ along a closed observation loop $\gamma$ produces a closed vineyard.  Utilizing tools from singularity theory, we show that the topology of this vineyard is controlled by the interaction of $\gamma$ with the generalized symmetry set of $\M$, the geometric discriminant formed by the union of focal and symmetry sets. We establish a Reidemeister-type theorem: under a generic deformation of $\gamma$, every topological change in the vineyard is generated by a finite collection of elementary local moves occurring when $\gamma$ passes codimension-two singularities of the generalized symmetry set.
Thus, despite the potentially unbounded global complexity of closed vineyards, their evolution is governed by a finite collection of local geometric events.

We classify these events and determine the corresponding changes to the vineyard, giving a complete local description of how radial vineyards evolve under generic deformation.  In addition, we  derive sufficient geometric conditions under which the resulting vineyard has no monodromy and trivial topology.
The 2D case already exhibits very rich behaviour, and we conjecture that in fact it captures all possible generic changes in vineyards.
